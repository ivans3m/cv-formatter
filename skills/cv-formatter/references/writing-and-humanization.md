# Reference: Writing, Impact Bullets & Anti-AI-Slop Rules

## Bullet Point Frameworks

- **Google XYZ**: *"Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]."*
- **CAR (Challenge → Action → Result)**: Useful for architectural, leadership, or turnaround bullets.
- **Front-load impact**: Lead with a concrete verb and measurable outcome so F-pattern scanners catch the value in the first 5–7 words.
- **Length discipline**:
  - 1–2 lines per bullet.
  - 3–5 bullets for the current/most recent role; 2–4 for mid-recent roles; 1–2 for older roles (>8–10 years old).

## Honest Quantification (Zero Hallucination)

- **Never fabricate numbers.** If the source has no metric, do not invent one.
- When exact revenue/percentages are unavailable, use truthful scope anchors from the source (team size, number of services/clients, request volume, latency, deployment frequency, hours saved).
- Never round concrete numbers into vague adjectives:
  - **Wrong**: *"Dramatically improved system reliability and throughput."*
  - **Right**: *"Maintained 99.95% SLA across 12 Go services handling 12,400 req/s."*

## Banned AI Clichés & Corporate Fog

Never use these phrases or patterns:
- *"Spearheaded transformative initiatives"*
- *"Seasoned, results-driven professional"*
- *"Leveraged cutting-edge solutions"*
- *"Orchestrated seamless cross-functional synergy"*
- *"Passionate self-starter"* / *"Dynamic fast-paced environment"*
- *"Testament to"*, *"delve"*, *"tapestry"*, *"seamlessly elevate"*, *"meticulously crafted"*
- Passive openers: *"Responsible for..."*, *"Duties included..."*, *"Helped with..."*

## Preferred Plain, Concrete Verbs

`Built`, `Designed`, `Led`, `Reduced`, `Cut`, `Migrated`, `Shipped`, `Automated`, `Scaled`, `Architected`, `Launched`, `Audited`, `Refactored`, `Negotiated`, `Mentored`, `Owned`, `Replaced`, `Consolidated`.

## Voice Check Before Finalizing

1. Vary verb choices and sentence rhythm (avoid starting 4 consecutive bullets with the same verb or identical 20-word cadence).
2. Keep technical nouns and domain terms exact.
3. Read the `## Summary` aloud—if it could describe 10,000 strangers, cut the fluff and replace it with concrete domain, years, and core strengths.
